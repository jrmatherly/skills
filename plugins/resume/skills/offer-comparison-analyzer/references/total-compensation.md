# Total compensation

## Components

**Cash**

- Base salary
- Signing bonus (one-time)
- Annual bonus (target %)
- Commission (sales roles)
- Relocation assistance (one-time)

**Equity**

- RSUs: paper value = current share price x number of shares
- Stock options: paper value = (current share price - strike price) x number
  of options; zero when the price is at or below the strike
- Vesting schedule and cliff
- Expected refresh grants

**Benefits**

- Health insurance (the employer's contribution)
- 401(k) or other retirement match
- HSA/FSA contributions
- Life and disability insurance
- Other insurance benefits

**Perks**

- Vacation days: value an extra day at about base salary / 260 working days
- Remote work: the commute and meal costs it saves, by the user's estimate
- Professional development budget
- Equipment or office stipend
- Meals, gym and similar

## Valuation notes

- Use the target bonus, and note whether it is guaranteed, discretionary or
  "up to".
- When annualizing equity, mention the cliff: nothing vests before it. With an
  uneven schedule, use what actually vests in the first 12 months for the
  year-1 total and grant / years for the ongoing figure.
- "Unlimited" vacation has no fixed dollar value. Ask how much time people
  actually take and compare on that.
- All figures are pre-tax. RSUs, options, bonuses and salary can be taxed
  differently, and rates differ by location, so equal totals may not mean
  equal take-home pay. Flag this for a tax professional.

## Worked example

The numbers are made up to show the layout and arithmetic. They are not
market data.

```
OFFER A - TOTAL COMPENSATION

CASH
Base salary:                     $150,000
Signing bonus (year 1 only):      $25,000
Target bonus (15%):               $22,500
---------------------------------
Cash compensation:               $197,500 (year 1)
                                 $172,500 (ongoing)

EQUITY
RSU grant: $200,000 over 4 years
---------------------------------
Equity compensation:              $50,000/year

BENEFITS
401(k) match (4%):                 $6,000
Health insurance:                 $15,000 (employer portion)
HSA contribution:                  $1,000
---------------------------------
Benefits value:                   $22,000/year

PERKS
Vacation: 20 days (vs 10 standard)
  Extra 10 days x ~$575/day:       $5,750
Remote work savings:               $3,000 (commute, lunch)
Professional development:          $2,000
---------------------------------
Perks value:                      $10,750/year

TOTAL YEAR 1:                    $280,250
TOTAL ONGOING:                   $255,250/year
```

Side by side with a second illustrative offer:

| Component               | Company A | Company B | Notes     |
| ----------------------- | --------- | --------- | --------- |

| **Cash total (year 1)** | $197,500  | $186,000  | A +$11.5K |
| **Equity**              |           |           |           |
| Grant value (4 years)   | $200,000  | $300,000  | B +$100K  |
| Annual equity           | $50,000   | $75,000   | B +$25K   |
| **Benefits**            |           |           |           |
| 401(k) match            | 4%        | 6%        | B +$3.6K  |
| Health insurance        | $15,000   | $15,000   | Same      |
| HSA contribution        | $1,000    | $0        | A +$1K    |
| **Benefits total**      | $22,000   | $24,600   | B +$2.6K  |
| **Perks**               |           |           |           |
| PTO                     | 20 days   | Unlimited | Not valued for B |
| Perks total             | $10,750   | $0        | A +$10.75K |
| **Total comp (year 1)** | $280,250  | $285,600  | B +$5.35K |

Reading it: B leads by about $5K in year 1 but by about $20K a year after
that, because A's lead is mostly its one-time signing bonus. More of B's
total is equity, so it carries more risk.
