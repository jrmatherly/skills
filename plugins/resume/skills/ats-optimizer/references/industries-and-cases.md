# Industries and special cases

## Industry emphasis

| Industry | What carries weight |
| --- | --- |
| Tech | Programming languages and frameworks by name. Certifications and courses. GitHub and portfolio links, placed in the body of the resume rather than the page header |
| Business and finance | Software proficiency (Excel, SAP, Salesforce). Certifications (CPA, CFA, PMP). Industry terms (P&L, ROI, KPI) |
| Healthcare | Licenses and certifications, which are usually hard requirements. Specific systems (Epic, Cerner, MEDITECH). Compliance terms (HIPAA, Joint Commission) |
| Marketing | Platform expertise (HubSpot, Salesforce, Google Analytics). Channel terms (SEO, PPC, email marketing). Metrics and results language |

These lists are starting points. The posting in front of you decides which
terms matter. For engineering resumes in depth, use the
**tech-resume-optimizer** skill.

## Career changers

- Lead with transferable skills.
- Take keywords from the target industry, not only the current one, wherever
  the candidate's experience genuinely maps to them.
- Two resume versions may be needed, one per target. Use the
  **career-changer-translator** skill for the translation itself.

## Recent graduates

- The education section carries more of the keyword load.
- Include relevant coursework and projects.
- Internships count as experience: put their keywords in experience bullets.

## Executive level

- Senior applications still pass through an ATS.
- Favor strategic keywords.
- Include board experience, P&L size and team size. Use the
  **executive-resume-writer** skill for the document itself.

## Gaps in employment

- Years without months are acceptable for dates when that reads better,
  applied consistently to every role. The dates stay true.
- Freelance and consulting work in the period is experience: include it with
  its keywords.
- Volunteer work can carry relevant keywords too.
