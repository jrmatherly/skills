# Red flags, positive signals and company research

Phrases in a posting are signals to interpret and probe, not proof. Report
each flag with how often it appears and what it may mean, and rate the overall
concern (none, minor, serious).

## Warning phrases

| Area         | Phrase                                | May mean                     |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Workload     | "Wear many hats"                      | Understaffed, unclear scope  |
| Workload     | "Fast-paced environment"              | Long hours, ambiguity, rapid change |
| Workload     | "Hit the ground running"              | Little onboarding            |
| Workload     | "Self-starter in ambiguous situations" | Little direction or support |
| Culture      | "Rockstar", "ninja", "guru"           | Unrealistic expectations     |
| Culture      | "We work hard, play hard"             | Long hours expected          |
| Culture      | "Unlimited vacation"                  | Time off may be discouraged  |
| Culture      | "Like a family"                       | Weak boundaries              |
| Compensation | "Competitive salary"                  | Range withheld               |
| Compensation | "Equity-heavy"                        | Low cash compensation        |
| Compensation | "Commission-based"                    | No base salary               |
| Compensation | "DOE" with no range                   | Range withheld               |

A very wide salary range may mean the experience level is flexible or that
there is room to negotiate; note it rather than flag it.

## Positive signals

- A detailed, well-organized description: the company knows what it wants.
- Specific tools named: a sign of operational maturity.
- Stated flexibility (hybrid, remote): modern workplace practices.

## Company research to recommend

Have the user check, or research with current sources when tools allow:

- Employee reviews (Glassdoor or similar): look for patterns in the lowest
  ratings, not single complaints.
- Recent news and funding: layoffs or growth.
- Tenure on LinkedIn: how long people in the team stay.
- Pay data (Levels.fyi or similar): whether the posted range is in line with
  the market.

Report findings with their source and date. If nothing was researched, list
these as open items rather than guessing.
