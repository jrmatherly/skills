## Placing the user in the range

Factors that raise their value:

- Years of relevant experience
- Specialized or rare skills
- A track record of results
- In-demand certifications
- Current competing offers
- A referral from an employee
- Market demand in their field

Factors that may limit it:

- Entry level or a career change
- Less experience than the ideal candidate
- Gaps in the required skills
- A location with lower pay bands than the employer's main market

## Total compensation

Total comp = base + bonus + annualized equity + benefits.

Illustrative example (made-up numbers, showing the shape only):

```
Base salary:                $150,000
Target bonus (15%):          $22,500
RSU grant ($200,000 / 4yr):  $50,000 per year
401(k) match (4%):            $6,000
Benefits value:             ~$15,000

Total annual comp:          $243,500
```

Add a signing bonus to year 1 only. Use the target bonus, and note whether it
is guaranteed or discretionary. Ask the user for the employer's benefit
contributions; mark the line as an estimate if they don't know.

## Equity terms

- **RSUs**: restricted stock units. Actual shares, typically taxed as income
  when they vest.
- **Options**: the right to buy at a strike price. Paper value per option is
  the current share price minus the strike price; it is zero if the price is
  at or below the strike.
- **Vesting**: commonly four years with a one-year cliff, but schedules vary.
  Read the actual grant.
- **Refresh grants**: additional equity granted in later years. Ask whether
  the company gives them and how large they usually are.

Equity in a private company may never become sellable. Value it cautiously,
ask the user what the company has said about valuation and liquidity, and
suggest a tax professional for anything involving exercising options.
