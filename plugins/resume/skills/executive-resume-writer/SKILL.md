# Executive resume writer

Write a resume for a C-suite, VP or Director candidate that tells decision
makers what the candidate transformed, at what scale, and how they lead.

This skill covers only what is specific to executive resumes. For general
bullet-writing technique use the **bullet-writer** skill and for layout and
ATS formatting the **formatter** skill.

## How an executive resume differs

| Standard resume | Executive resume |
| --- | --- |
| Lists skills | Demonstrates a leadership brand |
| Shows tasks | Shows strategic impact |
| Focuses on "what" | Focuses on "so what" |
| 1-2 pages | 2-3 pages acceptable |
| Keywords for ATS | Story for decision makers |
| Individual contributions | Organizational transformation |

The readers are executive recruiters, board members, PE/VC partners, CEOs and
CHROs. Optimize for a quick scan by a busy executive: a clear narrative of
career progression, an obvious leadership brand, and achievements that are
credible and verifiable.

## Inputs to gather

- The target role and the kind of company (size, stage, industry), and the
  job or search brief if there is one.
- Full career history. For each leadership role: company context (revenue,
  headcount, stage), reporting line and direct reports, P&L or budget owned,
  the mandate the user was hired for, and what changed under them.
- Results with figures: revenue growth, margin or EBITDA improvement, cost
  reduction, team growth, geographic expansion, M&A activity, exit outcomes.
- Board, advisory and observer roles; education and executive programs;
  industry recognition.
- What is confidential: figures, deal names or strategies that must not be
  disclosed.
- How the user describes their own leadership style and what they are known
  for. Ask; do not make up a philosophy for them.

## Rules

- **Truthfulness.** Never invent employers, titles, dates, degrees, metrics,
  scope or board seats. An estimated number must be derived from facts the
  user gave, labelled as an estimate and confirmed with the user before it
  goes into the resume. Executive searches commonly check references before
  interviews and fact-check the resume, so every claim must survive that.
- **Confidentiality.** When absolutes are confidential, use percentage
  improvements, ranges for revenue and headcount, or relative impact. Never
  include proprietary strategies, names of confidential acquisition targets
  or undisclosed financial information. Ask when unsure whether a figure is
  public.
- **Transformation over maintenance.** Executives are hired to change things.
  Every role must show what changed because of the user: the before state,
  the after state and the scale.
- **Tone.** Strategic verbs, no first-person pronouns, no unexplained jargon,
  no generic claims without proof, and no false modesty: take credit where it
  is due. Avoid tactical verbs such as "managed", "handled" and "assisted".
  For verb and phrase lists, see
  [references/language-and-framing.md](references/language-and-framing.md).

## Length

- VP/SVP: 2 pages.
- C-suite: 2-3 pages.
- Board CV: may be longer.

## Process

Work through the sections in this order. For a worked example of each, see
[references/section-examples.md](references/section-examples.md).

1. **Find the narrative.** Before writing, state in one line each: the key
   narrative theme, the leadership brand and the differentiator. Confirm them
   with the user; every section below must support them.
2. **Executive profile.** This replaces the standard summary and carries the
   leadership brand:
   - a headline of title or function plus two or three specialties;
   - a brand statement saying who the user is as a leader;
   - two or three sentences of track record with scope and scale;
   - what the user is known for, or their core philosophy.
3. **Core competencies.** Leadership themes in a compact grid (for
   example "P&L Management ($500M+)", "M&A Integration", "Board Relations"),
   not a list of tactical skills. If the resume will go through an applicant
   tracking system, render the grid as plain delimited lines per the
   **formatter** skill.
4. **Career highlights.** Four to six of the biggest achievements, each with
   metrics, placed before the chronological experience.
5. **Professional experience.** For each role include, in order: title;
   company, location and dates; a company context line (revenue, employees,
   stage); a short scope paragraph giving the mandate, reporting structure or
   span of control, and P&L or budget; then labelled initiative bullets
   written with the executive bullet formula:

   ```
   [Leadership action] + [strategic initiative] + [business outcome at scale]
   ```

   Where a role was a turnaround or change mandate, tell it with the
   transformation formula: "Inherited [situation]. Implemented [strategic
   change]. Achieved [outcome]."
6. **Frame tenure.** Present a short tenure as a specific mission and a long
   one as progression and reinvention; phrasings are in
   [references/language-and-framing.md](references/language-and-framing.md).
   The framing must match what actually happened.
7. **Board and advisory roles.** One entry per seat: role, organization,
   dates, then a line of company context and any committee role or
   contribution.
8. **Education and executive development.** Degrees and executive education.
   Add industry recognition as an optional final section.
9. **Show how the user leads.** Work one or two leadership-philosophy lines
   into the profile or scope paragraphs, in the user's own terms.
10. **Produce the resume.** Before producing output, read
    [templates/executive-resume.md](templates/executive-resume.md) and follow
    it.

## Before finishing

- Each role states company context, span of control and P&L or budget.
- Each highlight and initiative bullet carries a metric the user supplied or
  confirmed, and nothing confidential is disclosed.
- The profile, highlights and experience tell the same story.
- Remind the user that the resume opens the door: executive hiring also runs
  on references, relationships and reputation.
