# Academic CV builder

Build or update a curriculum vitae for an academic post. A CV is a complete
scholarly record, not a selective pitch, so the work is ordering, formatting
and completeness rather than cutting.

Before producing output, read
[templates/academic-cv-output.md](templates/academic-cv-output.md) and follow
it.

## How a CV differs from a resume

| Resume | Academic CV |
| --- | --- |
| 1-2 pages | 2-20+ pages, growing with the career |
| Highlights relevant experience | Comprehensive record |
| Results-focused | Scholarship-focused |
| Industry keywords | Disciplinary expertise |
| Skills section prominent | Publications prominent |
| Education minimal | Education detailed |

The **formatter** skill's rules for fonts, spacing, consistency and file
format still apply. Its page-length limits and default section order do not:
use the order below, and never trim a CV to fit a page count.

## Inputs to gather

- Target position type, institution and discipline, and the existing CV if
  there is one.
- Contact details, including department address, website and ORCID.
- All degrees, with dissertation or thesis title, advisor(s), committee
  (PhD), honors, minors or certificates.
- Appointments and research positions with dates, advisors and labs.
- Publications in every status: published, in press, under review, in
  preparation.
- Presentations, grants (agency, mechanism, role, dates, amounts), teaching,
  mentoring, service, memberships, honors.
- Any citation style or CV format the field, the institution or the job
  advertisement requires.

## Truthfulness

Never invent or upgrade a degree, appointment, publication, author order,
publication status, grant, grant role, amount, course, enrollment figure or
award. "In preparation" is not "under review", and "under review" is not "in
press". Use only what the user supplies, and list anything missing under
"Things to add/update" instead of filling it in.

## Section order

The usual order:

1. Contact information
2. Education
3. Research/academic positions
4. Publications
5. Presentations
6. Grants and funding
7. Teaching experience
8. Mentoring
9. Service
10. Professional memberships
11. Honors and awards
12. References (or "Available upon request")

Move sections to match the post:

- **Research position:** publications, grants and research experience first.
- **Teaching position:** teaching and course development first.
- **Administrative position:** leadership and service first.

For the layout of every section, with examples and the fields each entry
needs, see [references/section-formats.md](references/section-formats.md).

## Emphasis by role

| Role | Emphasize, in order |
| --- | --- |
| Tenure-track faculty | Publications (recent, high-impact); grants (especially independent funding); research trajectory and vision; teaching experience; mentoring record |
| Postdoctoral position | Publications from PhD and postdoc; research experience and skills; collaboration experience; future research potential; funding and fellowships |
| Lecturer / teaching faculty | Teaching experience (courses, evaluations); course development; pedagogical training; mentoring undergraduates; teaching awards |
| Research scientist | Publications; technical skills; grant-writing experience; collaboration record; relevant research experience |

Author order, publication types and what counts for most differ between the
sciences, humanities and social sciences: see
[references/discipline-conventions.md](references/discipline-conventions.md).
When the user's field has its own norm (citation style, chronological versus
reverse-chronological lists, listing unfunded grants), ask the user or check
a current CV from their department instead of assuming.

## Length

Typical lengths by career stage. A CV grows throughout a career: do not pad
it, and do not constrain it artificially.

| Career stage | Typical length |
| --- | --- |
| Graduate student | 2-4 pages |
| Postdoc | 3-6 pages |
| Early-career faculty | 5-10 pages |
| Mid-career faculty | 10-20 pages |
| Senior faculty | 15-30+ pages |

## Process

1. Gather the inputs; identify the role type and discipline.
2. Set the section order for the role, dropping sections with no content.
3. Format each section from the reference, in the field's citation style,
   applying one style consistently across the whole document.
4. Order entries reverse-chronologically unless the field expects otherwise.
5. Record field-specific choices in the formatting notes.
6. List what is missing or stale under "Things to add/update".
7. Run the checks below.

## Before finishing

- Contact information is complete, with ORCID where applicable.
- Education lists all degrees, advisors and dissertation titles.
- Publications are consistently formatted, with the user's name highlighted.
- Every grant shows the user's role and the amount.
- Teaching experience is comprehensive; service is documented.
- Formatting is consistent throughout.
- There are no unexplained gaps in the chronology; ask about any found.
- The CV reflects the last six months; ask what has changed since the last
  update.
