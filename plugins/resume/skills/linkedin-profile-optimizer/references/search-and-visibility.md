# Keywords, completeness and visibility

Thresholds, setting names and ranking behavior here are working heuristics,
not published guarantees. LinkedIn does not document its search ranking and
renames settings over time; confirm setting names against the current
product.

## Finding keywords

1. Search job descriptions for the target role.
2. Look at profiles of people already in the roles the user wants.
3. Use the suggestions LinkedIn offers in the Skills section.
4. Check industry publications for terminology.

## Placing keywords

In priority order:

1. Headline (highest weight)
2. About section (several times, naturally)
3. Experience descriptions
4. Skills section
5. Recommendations (ask recommenders to use them)

Search heuristics:

- Use the exact phrases recruiters search for; exact matches matter.
- Repeat the most important terms across sections, in natural sentences.
- Recent activity and engagement raise visibility.
- Complete profiles rank higher than partial ones.

## Completeness checklist

Baseline:

- Professional photo
- Custom headline, not just the job title
- Current position with a description
- Two past positions
- Education
- At least 5 skills
- Industry and postal code
- 50+ connections

Beyond the baseline:

- Custom background banner
- Featured section populated
- About section of 1,500+ characters
- Rich media in Experience
- 500+ connections
- 5+ recommendations
- A full, relevant skills list
- Volunteer experience
- Certifications

## Recruiter visibility settings

### Open to Work

Configure:

- job titles of interest
- location preferences
- start date
- job types (full-time, contract and so on)

Visibility options:

- **All LinkedIn members**: shows the green photo badge.
- **Recruiters only**: no badge, more discreet. Recommend this when the user
  is employed and the search is confidential, and tell them LinkedIn does not
  guarantee that recruiters at their own company cannot see it.

### Profile visibility

Check that:

- profile viewing options show the full profile
- sharing of profile edits is set to the user's preference (off while quietly
  rewriting a profile avoids notifying their network)
- representation in LinkedIn Services is on, if relevant

## Content strategy

Posting increases profile visibility, demonstrates expertise, builds a
personal brand and attracts opportunities.

Content types:

1. Industry insights and opinions
2. Professional lessons learned
3. Career milestones
4. Helpful resources
5. Engagement with others' content

Rule-of-thumb cadence:

- Minimum: once a week
- Better: 3-5 times a week
- Commenting and engaging: daily
